# Viral vs Bacterial Infection Classifier from Gene-Expression Profiles

Build the best binary classifier you can to distinguish viral from bacterial infection from pediatric peripheral-blood gene-expression profiles, and investigate why it works. This is a modelling and analysis task: strong cross-validated performance alone is not enough — you must also identify the genes that drive the classifier, reconcile them with the known host-response biology, and reconcile the bundled dataset with its published record. You will deliver three files: a reusable modelling pipeline (`pipeline.py`), a directional marker table (`markers.json`), and a model card (`report.md`).

## Data

The gene-expression series is shipped at `/workspace/data/GSE6269_family.soft.gz` — the full GEO SOFT family file for NCBI GEO series `GSE6269`. Parse it with `GEOparse.get_GEO(filepath="/workspace/data/GSE6269_family.soft.gz")`. The file contains three Affymetrix platforms; this task uses only `GPL96`. The platform annotation table (`gse.gpls["GPL96"].table`) maps probes to genes, and you will need it both to engineer features and to talk about specific genes.

After restricting to `GPL96`, derive labels from each sample's `characteristics_ch1` metadata: influenza A is viral (label `1`, 18 samples); *E. coli*, *S. aureus*, or *S. pneumoniae* is bacterial (label `0`, 73 samples); healthy controls and all other phenotypes are excluded. This yields exactly 91 labelled samples, and your pipeline must return features for these 91 and no others.

Internet access is available; use it to look up the GEO series record and its linked publication so you can reconcile them with the bundled file in your report.

## Deliverables

Write `/workspace/output/pipeline.py` exposing two callables. `build_features(gse)` receives the parsed GEOparse object (containing all platforms) and returns a tuple `(X, sample_ids, feature_names)`, where `X` is a 2-D array-like of shape `(91, n_features)`, `sample_ids` is the list of 91 GSM accession strings row-aligned to `X`, and `feature_names` is a list of length `n_features` giving the gene symbol of each column, column-aligned to `X`. The set of `sample_ids` must equal the 91 labelled samples. Feature engineering is up to you (probe-to-gene mapping, normalisation, transforms, selection), but `feature_names` must let a reader tie each column back to a gene, and `build_features` does not receive labels. `make_estimator()` returns an unfitted scikit-learn-compatible estimator implementing `fit(X, y)` and `predict_proba(X)`; put any label-dependent steps inside it so they are refit per fold. The module must import cleanly and must not require network access.

Write `/workspace/output/markers.json`, a JSON object with two lists: `viral_up_markers` (genes higher in viral samples) and `bacterial_up_markers` (genes higher in bacterial samples). Each list must contain at least 8 gene symbols, every symbol must appear in your `feature_names`, no gene may appear in both lists, and the directions must be correct. Derive these from the model's weights, a differential-expression analysis, or both. Extra keys are allowed and ignored.

Write `/workspace/output/report.md`, a model card explaining your scientific choices. It is reviewed by a human and is not machine-scored, so write it for a reviewer rather than a regex. Cover your feature engineering and its robustness to probe-to-gene mapping choices; your leakage controls; per-fold cross-validation variance and feature stability; calibration of the predicted probabilities; the biological plausibility of the top predictors (comparing viral markers against the known type-I interferon response and bacterial markers against the neutrophil response); and a reconciliation of the bundled SOFT file with the external GEO/PubMed record for `GSE6269` (the linked publication, the full multi-platform sample count, and how it relates to the 91-sample GPL96 cohort you model).

## Grading

The verifier owns the cohort, the labels, the cross-validation splits, and the scoring, so you cannot inflate your score by training on held-out data. It imports your module, calls `build_features` once, derives the true labels itself, and runs its own `StratifiedKFold(n_splits=5, shuffle=True, random_state=123)`. You pass only if the cohort and the `(X, sample_ids, feature_names)` contract are satisfied; the mean held-out AUC-ROC is at least 0.90 with the worst single fold at least 0.80; the mean held-out Brier score is at most 0.15; every reported marker is a real feature, directionally correct, and overlaps the canonical interferon or neutrophil signature in at least 3 genes per class; and a simple classifier built from only your reported markers still reaches a mean CV AUC of at least 0.85.
